1. The product in one page

A product Talent Carriage sells to employers. It watches attendance data, talks to the employees whose attendance is incomplete, and finishes the paperwork for them.

1.1 The problem it removes

Every month, HR teams spend days chasing the same thing: employees marked absent, on half-day, or with a missed punch, who have not applied for leave or regularisation. Payroll cannot close until each one is explained and approved. Today it is done with phone calls, hand-typed WhatsApp messages and spreadsheets — slow, repetitive, and badly recorded.

1.2 What the agent does instead

  • It notices. Each day, at a time the customer sets, it reads that day's attendance and finds every gap: absent, half-day, missed punch, or any other status that customer treats as needing an explanation.
  • It asks. One WhatsApp message per gap, in the employee's language, with buttons to reply.
  • It talks. Employees can reply with a button, a number, or plain typed text — and can ask questions back: how much leave they have left, what the regularisation deadline is, what to do if they were at a client site. The agent answers from that employer's own policy.
  • It acts. This is what makes it an agent rather than a reminder tool. If leave or regularisation needs applying, it asks the manager to approve on WhatsApp and then performs the action in the customer's HRMS.
  • It chases. Still pending after two days? A reminder. Still pending on day three? An automated phone call that holds a short spoken conversation and records the outcome.
  • It reports. Every case, message, approval and action, with a full audit trail and clean month-end numbers.

1.4 Why it can be sold to many different employers

No two employers record attendance the same way. One writes A|A for a full-day absence and P|A for a second-half absence; another writes Ab, CL, SL. One stores Full name, another First name and Last name. One has a modern HRMS with an API, another exports an Excel file daily. The platform therefore keeps everything customer-specific in a mapping layer, configured once during onboarding. Detection, messaging, AI, actions and reporting are then identical for every customer — one product, sold repeatedly, not rebuilt per employer. Section 3 explains it.

2. How it works, end to end

One employee, one date with a gap, followed until it is closed — the flow from the agreed process flowchart, with conversation and action steps added.

Day 0. The platform pulls the day's attendance, translates it through that customer's mapping, and creates cases — one case is one employee and one date. The employee gets a single, clearly dated WhatsApp message with reply buttons. Three open dates means three separate conversations, never a confusing bundle.

Reply, discussion and action. The four standard answers from the agreed flow (I was absent, I was working, already applied leave, already sent regularisation) each get the correct instruction immediately. The employee can also ask questions, and where the customer has enabled actions the agent offers to do the work: “Shall I apply casual leave for 18-Aug and send it to Deepak for approval?”

Day 2. Before any reminder, the HRMS is re-checked. If the leave was applied and approved meanwhile, the case closes quietly — no nagging. Otherwise the reminder is worded for that person's situation.

Day 3. Cases still open are called. The call is a short spoken conversation, not a recorded announcement: the employee can say what happened, ask a question, or ask for HR. Outcomes route to HR with a recommended next step.

3. Built to fit any customer

The heart of the product. Each customer's data differs; the agent's logic must not. Three kinds of mapping make that possible, all done by filling in a screen rather than writing code.

An unmapped code never triggers a message. Silence is safe; guessing is not. The admin screen shows a live count of unmapped codes from the last import, so gaps surface immediately.

3.3 Policy and rules — “how does this employer work?”

Per-customer settings, with sensible defaults, most also settable per department or location: daily check time and time zone · which meanings to chase · grace periods and thresholds · quiet hours and working days · holiday and shift calendars · who to exclude (new joiners, exits, long leave, named roles) · escalation timings (reminder day, call day, retries) · approval chain and timeouts · languages offered · which actions the agent may perform and which leave types it may apply · message wording and signature. Alongside these sits the policy pack: the customer's attendance, leave and regularisation rules in their own words, used to answer employees' questions (section 6.3).

3.4 The mapping studio

  1. Bring a sample. Upload a real file, or connect the HRMS and pull a few days.
  2. Auto-detect. The platform lists the columns and the distinct status codes found, with counts, and AI proposes what each one means. A human confirms.
  3. Fill the gaps. Anything unmapped is highlighted and must be decided before go-live.
  4. Dry run. It shows exactly who would have been messaged for a past date, and the exact wording, with nothing sent. This is how a customer signs off the configuration.
  5. Save as a profile. Versioned with an audit trail, and clonable as the starting point for the next customer on the same HRMS — which is what shortens each onboarding to a review.

5. The Action Engine

The difference between a reminder tool and an agent: the employee asks, the manager approves, and the platform performs the action in the HRMS and proves it did.

Employee: taps Yes, I was absent
Agent: “You have 6 casual leave days available. Shall I apply casual leave for 18-Aug-2026 and send it to Deepak for approval?”  [ Yes, apply it ] [ I'll do it myself ] [ Different leave type ]
Employee: taps Yes, apply it
Agent → Manager: “Ankit Panwar has requested casual leave for 18-Aug-2026. Reason: fever. Balance after approval: 5 days.”  [ Approve ] [ Reject ] [ Ask for details ]
Manager: taps Approve
Agent: applies it in the HRMS, then confirms to both with the HRMS reference. Case closed.

5.1 How an action is defined

Actions are definitions the platform executes, not one-off code, so new ones can be added later without redesign. Each has a trigger (an intent, a case state, or an HR click), inputs (date, half or full, leave type, reason), pre-checks (balance, backdating window, overlaps, probation, blackout dates), an approval step (who, in what order, with what timeout and fallback), execution (the connector call or a fallback route), proof (the HRMS reference stored on the case), failure handling and notifications. The v1 catalogue is in Appendix A.

5.2 Rules the engine holds itself to

  • Never act without a mandate. An explicit employee request, plus an explicit approval where configured. An ambiguous reply never results in a write.
  • Never act twice. Every action carries an idempotency key, so a retry, a duplicate WhatsApp delivery or a double tap cannot create two leave requests.
  • Check before writing. Balance, eligibility and duplicate checks run first, so the employee is told why in plain language instead of a failure appearing later.
  • Prove it. The HRMS's own reference is stored and shown. If nothing verifiable comes back, the action is marked unconfirmed and read back on the next sync rather than assumed successful.
  • Fail loudly, to a person. A failed write retries, then lands in an HR queue with the error and the suggested fix. Never silently dropped.

5.3 When the HRMS cannot be written to

Some HRMS products have no write API, or IT will not allow one initially. The conversation and approval flow stay the same, ending in one of these instead: a daily approved-request file in the HRMS's import format · a structured ticket or email to HR with the approval attached · an HR action queue in our portal where HR performs it and confirms · or guidance only, with completion verified from the next data sync.

6. WhatsApp: a real conversation

WhatsApp is the employee's interface: no app to install, no login, no training. Beyond the buttons, the agent holds a genuine conversation about attendance and the employer's policies.

6.1 The channel mechanics

  • The first message must be a Meta-approved template. Each customer's templates are submitted and tracked in the platform, with approval status visible.
  • After the employee replies, we can talk freely for 24 hours. All discussion, offers, approvals and confirmations happen inside that window; after it closes, the next contact is a template again.
  • Meta charges per conversation, not per message (section 16). Opt-out is permanent, and those employees are routed to HR instead.
  • The sending number is the customer's own, so employees see their employer. Either the customer's Meta account is connected, or — recommended for selling at scale — Talent Carriage becomes a Meta Tech Provider and the customer connects their number through a short guided flow in our portal.

6.2 What the conversation can cover

The agent is a subject-matter assistant for attendance, not a general chatbot. Within that subject it can:

Employee saysAgent does
“I was on client site that day”Recognises a regularisation case, confirms the date and reason, offers to apply it
“How many leaves do I have left?”Reads the live balance from the HRMS and answers, by leave type
“Till when can I regularise August?”Answers from that employer's policy pack, quoting the actual rule
“No casual leave left, what now?”Explains the employer's unpaid-leave or regularisation route, then offers to start it
“Mera manager chhutti pe hai”Applies the approval fallback: alternate approver or HR, per configuration
“Talk to HR”Hands the case to HR with the transcript, and tells the employee who will follow up
Anything outside attendance (salary, appraisal, IT)Politely declines and points to the right HR channel. It does not improvise.

6.3 Where the policy answers come from

Each customer's attendance, leave and regularisation policy is loaded as a policy pack — their own documents, plus short question-and-answer entries HR can edit in the portal. Answers are generated only from that pack and from live HRMS data, and the portal shows HR which clause each answer came from. If the pack does not cover a question, the agent says so and offers HR help rather than inventing a rule.

To be designed in detail
Exactly how policy content is ingested, indexed and kept current — and how much HR edits directly versus re-uploading a document — is a design exercise in Phase 3. The principles above (grounded answers, visible source, no invention, HR review before enabling) are fixed.

6.4 Guardrails

  • Approved wording starts every conversation; generated text only inside a live conversation, and only on attendance topics.
  • No promises the platform cannot keep: it never says leave is approved until the manager has approved and the HRMS confirms. No other employee's data, no salary or payroll advice, no disciplinary opinions.
  • Sensitive disclosures (illness, bereavement, harassment) get a short, human reply and go to HR; medical detail is not probed. Abuse or distress ends the automated exchange and escalates.
  • Every generated message is logged with the model used, and HR can review a sample before conversational mode is enabled for a customer.

7. Voice: a real conversation

On day 3, unresolved cases are called. The call is a spoken exchange driven by the same agent brain as WhatsApp — not a recorded announcement with a keypad menu.

7.1 How the call works

  1. The platform places the call through the customer's voice provider, respecting quiet hours and retry limits.
  2. The agent introduces itself, states the pending date, and asks what happened.
  3. Speech to text transcribes the reply; the agent brain decides what to say next using the case, the HRMS data and the policy pack; text to speech speaks it back.
  4. The employee can answer naturally, ask a question (“how many leaves do I have?”), ask for a repeat, or ask for HR — in which case the call is transferred or an HR callback is logged.
  5. Where the employee prefers it or the line is poor, it falls back to keypad input (“press 1 if completed, 2 if not, 3 for HR”), so an outcome is always captured.
  6. The outcome, transcript and — where the customer enables it and the call announces it — the recording are stored on the case.

Conversation length is capped: anything unresolved after two turns goes to HR rather than looping. Target response time between the employee finishing a sentence and the agent replying is under two seconds.

7.2 Voice providers

One internal interface, so the provider can differ per customer or country:

Automated outbound calling in India carries consent and do-not-disturb obligations, so consent is captured at onboarding, calls respect quiet hours, and an employee can ask never to be called again.

8. The AI layer and model strategy

Several models, each used where it is strongest, behind one internal interface — so a model can be swapped, priced or restricted per customer without touching the product.

8.1 Which model does what

8.2 The model gateway

  • One interface, many providers. The product asks to “classify this reply” or “answer this question”; the gateway picks the model based on language, task and the customer's configuration.
  • Per-customer choice. A customer who mandates Indian-only processing can be pinned to Sarvam and an Indian-region deployment; another may prefer a single vendor. No code change either way.
  • Automatic fallback. If a provider is slow or erroring, the gateway retries on the alternate model; if all fail, the case goes to HR rather than staying silent.
  • Cost control. Rules first, short prompts, small models, cached policy answers, per-customer spend caps and alerts.
  • Evaluation before switching. A saved set of real replies and policy questions is re-run against any new model or prompt, and accuracy compared, before it becomes the default.
  • No training on customer data, contractually with every provider; only the text needed for the decision is sent, never the employee database.

8.3 What the AI is not allowed to do

  • It does not decide who gets messaged. That comes from the mapping and rules — exact, reviewable logic.
  • It does not write to the HRMS. It proposes; the action engine validates and performs.
  • It does not invent policy or balances. Those are read from the policy pack and the HRMS.
  • It does not guess. Below the confidence threshold it asks one clarifying question, then hands over to a human with the original message attached.

Every AI decision is logged with the message, the intent, the confidence and the model version, and is reviewable in a queue where HR can correct it. Corrections become test cases. Accuracy, ask-again rate and handover rate are reported per customer, so the automation is measured rather than assumed.

Every import produces a report: rows read, employees matched, rows rejected with reasons, unmapped codes seen, and what changed since last time. Later corrections are picked up, and a case closes automatically if the correction explains the gap. Writing back uses the routes in section 5.3.

Connectors are small, standard pieces built to a fixed internal contract — fetch attendance, fetch employees, fetch leave balance, apply leave, apply regularisation, approve, read status — each declaring what its HRMS supports, so the agent only offers an employee what can actually be delivered. A generic file connector and a generic REST connector cover many customers through configuration alone; named connectors for common HRMS products are built as customers arrive and reused afterwards. Every connector has a sandbox mode that reads real data but writes nothing, and all calls are logged, rate-limited and retried, with alerts on repeated failure.

Practical onboarding path
Start read-only, run in guidance-only mode for a week or two so the customer trusts the detection and messaging, then enable write-back actions once their IT is comfortable.

One platform, many customers. Every record carries a customer identifier, enforced in the database with row-level security, so one customer's data cannot be reached through another's session — not merely filtered in application code. Credentials, configuration, mapping profiles, templates, calendars, policy packs and action permissions are all per customer. A large or regulated customer can be given a dedicated database without changing the application, and usage is metered per customer for billing and fair-use limits.

Environments. Development (synthetic data, sending disabled), staging (release testing and each customer's onboarding dry runs), production (with a per-customer test mode). Reliability details that matter in practice. Webhook events are acknowledged instantly and processed from a queue, so no reply is lost during a deployment. Duplicate protection throughout: one case per employee per date, one message per case, one action per request, deduplicated on provider message identifiers. A missed daily run catches up rather than skipping a day. Sends are paced per WhatsApp number so a month-end spike does not trip Meta's rate limits.

11. Security, privacy and compliance

The platform holds employee phone numbers, attendance records and personal reasons for absence, and is designed for that responsibility.

  • Access. Role-based: platform admin, HR admin, HR user, manager, auditor. Managers see only their team; HR users can be scoped by department or location. SSO for customer staff; enforced multi-factor authentication for our staff. Our support access to customer data is time-limited, reason-logged and visible to the customer.
  • Protection. TLS in transit, platform encryption at rest, encrypted backups. Secrets only in Key Vault, rotatable without downtime. Signature verification on every inbound WhatsApp webhook. Phone numbers and message content masked in logs. Dependency scanning, and an external penetration test before the first production customer.
  • Privacy roles and data. The customer is the data fiduciary; Talent Carriage processes on their instructions under a data processing agreement. Only the fields in section 3.1 are ingested — salary, bank and identity data are never requested.
  • Notice and consent. Onboarding includes an employee communication pack — what the agent is, what it sends, how to opt out of messages and calls. Consent and opt-out states are stored per employee.
  • Retention and residency. Configurable per customer; 24 months by default for cases, transcripts and recordings, then deleted or anonymised. Offboarding produces a full export followed by deletion. Data and AI processing stay in Indian regions by default, including the Indian-language models.
  • Employee rights. Access, correction and erasure requests are supported through the customer's HR, with tooling to find and export everything held about one person.
  • Channel rules. Approved templates only to start conversations; opt-outs permanent; no marketing; quiet hours on messages and calls; recording only where enabled and announced.
  • Audit trail. Each case keeps an unalterable history: the import row that created it, every message with delivery status, the AI's reading, confidence and model, each approval with who and when, each HRMS write with its reference or error, and every human intervention with a reason — exportable, and usable as evidence in a payroll dispute.

15. Assumptions, exclusions and risks

15.1 Assumptions and dependencies

Employees have WhatsApp on a mobile number the employer holds and may use for work · attendance data is available at least daily and is reasonably accurate (the agent explains gaps, it does not fix bad data) · each customer can state their attendance codes, leave types and approvers, and provide their policy in writing · managers are contactable on WhatsApp · customers accept WhatsApp, voice and AI usage charges as pass-through or bundled · India-first for launch: Indian numbers, Indian leave conventions, English, Hindi and Gujarati first. Outside our control: Meta template approvals, number verification and policy changes · the HRMS vendor's API and how fast access is granted · the customer's IT and HR providing samples, credentials, calendars and the employee communication · voice provider account approval and Indian calling clearances · model provider availability and pricing.

15.2 Explicitly out of scope

Replacing the HRMS or becoming the system of record · payroll processing or statutory filings · biometric devices and access-control hardware · shift rostering and workforce planning · performance management or disciplinary automation, and any decision affecting a person's employment (the agent gathers and records; people decide) · recruitment and unrelated HR workflows · a native employee mobile app (WhatsApp is the interface by design) · general HR chatbot duties beyond attendance, leave and regularisation.

16. Running costs and pricing levers

WhatsApp conversations (Meta)Charged per conversation, not per message, at Meta's country and category rates. Scales with the number of attendance gaps, not headcount. Follow-ups inside 24 hours are free — the flow is designed around that.
Voice callsPer minute (Plivo or Exotel), plus speech recognition and synthesis per minute. Only for cases still unresolved on day 3 — a small fraction of cases.
AI modelsOnly for replies that are not buttons or numbers, and for policy questions. Small models, short prompts, cached answers: a fraction of a rupee per interaction at current rates.
AzureApp Service, PostgreSQL, Functions, Service Bus, Blob, Key Vault, monitoring. Largely fixed, shared across customers, stepping up with volume.
Onboarding effortPeople time for mapping, integration and sign-off. Falls sharply once a profile exists for a given HRMS.
Support and successMonitoring health, connector breakages after customer HRMS upgrades, template and policy changes.

Pricing models this supports: per employee per month, tiered by headcount, with messaging bundled under fair use or passed through · platform fee plus metered usage · per resolved case, which ties most closely to value and makes a strong pilot offer · plus a one-time onboarding fee, and separate pricing for named HRMS connectors that need new development. The platform meters everything needed for any of these from Phase 5, and reports volumes per customer from Phase 2, so pricing decisions rest on real numbers.

Appendix A — Attendance model and action catalogue

A.1 Canonical attendance meanings

The fixed internal vocabulary every customer's codes map into, so the engine and reports stay consistent: present · absent full day · absent first half · absent second half · absent half-day unspecified · missed or incomplete punch · short hours · late in / early out · leave approved · leave pending approval · leave rejected · leave without pay · on duty, work from home, travel, training · compensatory off · weekly off · holiday · optional holiday · suspension · long leave · not employed on that date · unknown (unmapped). Chased by default: the absence, half-day and missed-punch meanings. Never chased: weekly off, holiday, not-employed, unknown. Configurable: short hours, lateness, leave without pay. Leave pending approval chases the manager rather than the employee.
